1 The Case, the Muscle, and the Acute Mobilisation

Learning objectives:

  • State the epidemiological case: regular physical activity is associated with lower cancer incidence, mortality and recurrence; ≈ half of all cancer deaths are attributable to modifiable lifestyle and environmental factors; and physical inactivity is at pandemic scale, with nearly one in three adults below WHO recommendations (Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).
  • Explain skeletal muscle as an endocrine organ: contraction releases myokines, chief among them IL-6, which rises exponentially during exercise and drives an anti-inflammatory response (induction of IL-1RA and IL-10, reduction of TNF), and can mobilise NK cells into the blood and into tumours.
  • Connect muscle mass to prognosis: sarcopenia (muscle wasting) is associated with worse outcomes in cancer patients, motivating the maintenance of muscle as itself an immuno-oncological goal.
  • Describe the acute-exercise mobilisation of effector lymphocytes with high cytotoxic potential — NK cells, CD8⁺ T cells, γδ T cells — and the NK killing mechanisms they deploy: granule exocytosis and TRAIL-mediated apoptosis, favoured against tumour cells by the absence of MHC-I (Murphy and Weaver 2016; Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).
  • Explain why the mobilised cells are of therapeutic interest: they show anti-tumour transcriptomic profiles and enhanced graft-versus-leukaemia effects in xenograft mouse models (Batatinha et al. 2023).
  • Trace the catecholamine link: the same β2-adrenergic mobilisation that drives the acute leukocytosis preferentially recruits the high-cytotoxicity effectors relevant to tumour surveillance.

1.1 Epidemiological Evidence

Regular physical activity is associated with lower cancer incidence and mortality as well as lower tumor recurrence rates. Approximately half of all cancer deaths could be prevented through modification of lifestyle and environmental factors. Physical inactivity has reached pandemic proportions — nearly one in three adults worldwide does not meet WHO recommendations (Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).

1.2 Muscle Tissue and Immunity

Skeletal muscle functions as an endocrine organ that releases myokines during contraction. IL-6 as the most prominent myokine increases exponentially during exercise and triggers an anti-inflammatory effect: induction of IL-1RA and IL-10, reduction of TNF. Muscular IL-6 can stimulate NK cells to be mobilized into the bloodstream and infiltrate tumors. Muscle wasting (sarcopenia) is associated with worse prognoses in cancer patients (Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).

1.3 Immune Effects of Acute Exercise on Cancer Immunity

Acute exercise preferentially mobilizes effector lymphocytes with high cytotoxic potential (NK cells, CD8⁺ T cells, γδ T cells). The mobilized NK cells utilize their described cytotoxic mechanisms — granule exocytosis and TRAIL-mediated apoptosis — for the recognition and destruction of malignantly transformed cells, with the absence of MHC-I on tumor cells favoring NK cell activation (Murphy and Weaver 2016). These cells show anti-tumor transcriptional profiles and enhanced graft-versus-leukemia effects in xenograft mouse models (Batatinha et al. 2023; Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).

One-Minute-Paper Topics

A One-Minute-Paper (OMP) is a short, focused prompt that students answer in ~60 seconds at the end of a session to consolidate learning, surface misconceptions, and provide formative feedback. When answering, be concise, specific, and use terminology from today’s session.

  • Summarise in one sentence the epidemiological evidence linking physical activity and reduced cancer risk.
  • Explain the role of myokines in the anti-cancer effects of exercise.
  • How does acute exercise mobilise NK cells, and why is this clinically interesting?
  • What is the role of CD8⁺ T cells in tumour immune surveillance?
  • Describe the function of γδ T cells and why they are relevant in exercise oncology.
  • Explain one mechanism by which IL-6 from skeletal muscle could affect tumour growth.
  • How do exercise-induced catecholamines influence NK cell trafficking?

2 Regular Training, the Tumour Microenvironment, and the Clinic

Learning objectives:

  • Describe how regular training delays tumour progression in preclinical models through four converging mechanisms: enhanced tumour infiltration of NK and CD8⁺ T cells (epinephrine-induced mobilisation, IL-6-mediated redistribution); reduction of immunosuppressive regulatory T cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells in the tumour microenvironment; improved T-cell function (reduced exhaustion, greater repertoire diversity); and metabolic reprogramming (muscle-derived lactate improving CD8⁺ T-cell stemness) (Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).
  • Explain the lactate–stemness link: lactate exported from working muscle is not waste but a signal that may enhance the stem-like, self-renewing properties of CD8⁺ T cells — connecting the lactate-shuttle framework directly to anti-tumour immunity.
  • Contrast the acute and chronic contributions: acute exercise supplies a transient surge of circulating effectors (§1), while regular training reshapes the resident immune landscape of the tumour itself — the two are complementary, not alternatives.
  • State the clinical-translation directions: immune cells harvested from post-exercise blood as a source for adoptive cell therapy, and the combination of physical activity with immunotherapies such as checkpoint inhibitors as a promising research frontier.
  • Distinguish exercise as prevention (the §1 epidemiology) from exercise as treatment (the §2.2 adjunct-therapy question), and articulate why the mechanisms differ.

2.1 Effects of Regular Training on Tumor Immunity

Preclinical studies show that regular training delays tumor progression. Mechanisms include:

  • Enhanced tumor infiltration: Increased infiltration of NK cells and CD8⁺ T cells into tumors through epinephrine-induced mobilization and IL-6-mediated redistribution
  • Reduction of regulatory T cells (Tregs): Decrease in immunosuppressive cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) in the tumor microenvironment
  • Improvement of T cell function: Reduction of T cell exhaustion and improvement of T cell repertoire diversity
  • Metabolic reprogramming: Lactate from working muscles may improve stemness properties of CD8⁺ T cells

2.2 Clinical Implications

There is increasing evidence that immune cells obtained from post-exercise blood could be used as adoptive cell therapy in cancer. The combination of physical activity with immunotherapies (e.g., checkpoint inhibitors) is a promising area of research (Fiuza-Luces et al. 2023).
